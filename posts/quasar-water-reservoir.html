The Quasar Holding 140 Trillion Oceans

Twelve billion light-years away, a quasar holds the largest water reservoir ever found — 140 trillion times Earth’s oceans. How astronomers weighed it.

By Emilio Lopez · September 19, 2026

In 2011, two teams of astronomers announced the largest body of water ever detected: a vapor cloud surrounding the quasar APM 08279+5255, 12 billion light-years away. Its mass equals roughly 140 trillion Earth oceans. The water isn’t liquid — it’s vapor spread across hundreds of light-years, glowing in wavelengths our eyes can’t see — but chemically, it’s H₂O, and there is more of it in that one spot than in every ocean, ice cap, and aquifer here combined. The measurement rests on observational data published by the discovery teams using the Z-Spec instrument.

Because the quasar’s light has traveled for 12 billion years, we’re seeing water as it existed when the universe was young — evidence that water, the prerequisite for life as we know it, was abundant almost from the start. The find is now catalogued in astronomical records as the record-holder, though astronomers expect bigger reservoirs are out there waiting for better instruments.

How we know: Bradford et al. (2011) and Lis et al. (2011), based on submillimeter spectroscopy of APM 08279+5255.
