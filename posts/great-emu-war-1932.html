The War Australia Lost to Birds

In 1932, Australia sent soldiers with machine guns against 20,000 emus. The emus won. The military records behind the Great Emu War.

By Emilio Lopez · September 19, 2026

In late 1932, some 20,000 emus migrated into Western Australia’s wheat country, trampling crops and knocking down fences. Desperate farmers — many of them war veterans — asked for military help. The government obliged: soldiers with Lewis machine guns were deployed under Major G.P.W. Meredith. What followed is preserved in military records of the campaign and it reads like a farce written by the birds themselves.

The emus scattered at the first burst, ran at 50 km/h in small groups, and seemed to absorb bullets without slowing; one gun jammed after a dozen kills. After a week, the troops withdrew. Meredith reportedly praised the emus’ maneuverability, noting they ‘face machine guns with the tanks’ resilience.’ Press coverage — contemporary newspaper accounts, archived and searchable — turned the whole affair into a national joke, and the government eventually solved the problem the unglamorous way: with fences.

How we know: Australian Army records of the 1932 operation, Meredith’s own remarks to the press, and newspaper archives from Perth and Sydney.
