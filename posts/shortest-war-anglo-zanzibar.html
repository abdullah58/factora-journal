The War That Lasted 38 Minutes

In 1896, Britain and Zanzibar fought the shortest recorded war in history — about 38 minutes. How we know, from naval logs and press reports.

By Emilio Lopez · September 19, 2026

On August 27, 1896, the Anglo-Zanzibar War began at 9:02 a.m. and was over by about 9:40. The cause was a succession dispute: Sultan Hamad had died, and his nephew Khalid seized the palace without British approval. Britain, which held a protectorate over Zanzibar, issued an ultimatum to vacate by 9:00 a.m. Khalid barricaded himself inside with 2,800 men, a few artillery pieces, and a royal yacht fitted with a single gun. The timeline is recorded in naval logs and contemporary press almost to the minute.

At 9:02, three British warships opened fire. The palace — mostly wooden — caught fire within minutes; the yacht was sunk; Khalid fled to the German consulate. Around 500 Zanzibaris were killed or wounded. The British suffered one casualty: a petty officer badly wounded in the leg. The whole affair is preserved in detailed historical entries because Victorian Britain documented its gunboat diplomacy meticulously — including the awkward fact that the ‘enemy fleet’ was a single pleasure yacht.

How we know: British Admiralty records, the ultimatum correspondence, and same-week reporting in the London press.
