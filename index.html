History

The officer who refused to end the world

On September 26, 1983, Soviet officer Stanislav Petrov watched his early-warning system report incoming American missiles. Protocol demanded a retaliatory strike. He looked at the data — only five launches, from a brand-new system he didn’t trust — and judged it a false alarm. He did nothing. He was right: a satellite had mistaken sunlight glinting off clouds for missile launches. One person’s refusal to follow procedure is the reason that night stayed ordinary.

How we know: declassified Soviet records, Petrov’s own testimony, and later analysis of the satellite’s sun-alignment error.

Nature

The town where it rains fish

In Yoro, Honduras, heavy downpours are followed by an impossible sight: small silver fish flapping on the soaked ground. The Lluvia de Peces — the Rain of Fish — has been reported for over a century. Scientists suspect underground rivers flood during storms and flush the fish up through fissures in the rock, but no expedition has ever fully traced their journey. The town, unbothered by the mystery, holds a festival for it every year.

How we know: eyewitness accounts going back generations, samples collected by visiting researchers, and hydrological surveys of the region’s cave systems.

Time

Cleopatra lived closer to the Moon landing than to the pyramids

The Great Pyramid was completed around 2560 BC. Cleopatra died in 30 BC — roughly two and a half millennia later. The Moon landing happened in 1969, less than sixty years ago. She lived nearer in time to us than to the monument builders of her own country, and the pyramids were already ancient ruins when she was born.

How we know: established Egyptian chronology from archaeological and textual records on both ends of the timeline.

Science

Sharks are older than Saturn’s rings

Sharks have patrolled the oceans for roughly 450 million years — they predate trees, dinosaurs, and the Atlantic Ocean. Saturn’s rings, by recent estimates from Cassini mission data, are only about 100 million years old: younger than the last dinosaurs. The most iconic feature of the solar system’s most iconic planet is, cosmically speaking, the newcomer.

How we know: the fossil record for early sharks; ring-age estimates from Cassini’s measurements of ring mass and purity.

Our method

Every entry follows
the same trail.

  1. Start with the claim. Each entry begins as a single sentence someone asserted — the bolder, the better.
  2. Chase the evidence. Primary sources, papers, archives, datasets. No fact survives on vibes alone.
  3. Show the trail. Every entry names what it leans on, so you can check our work instead of taking our word for it.
  4. Write it plain. Jargon gets translated or cut. If a curious twelve-year-old can’t follow it, it goes back for a rewrite.
